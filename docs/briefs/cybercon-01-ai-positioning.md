**Working Title (H1):** Demote AI in Your Positioning

**Meta Title:** The Headline Problem in AI Marketing
> The H1 leaves out the core keyword on purpose. The meta title carries it, so the H1 can stay short and opinionated.

**Target Persona:** CMOs and product marketing leads at B2B tech companies who own positioning and messaging. Cybersecurity vendors are the lead example, because that market shows the problem most clearly, but the argument applies to any B2B tech company adding AI to its story.

**Core Keyword:** AI marketing (meta title, then the exact-match phrase once in the body. Place it in the first ~100 words and work it into one H2 if it fits naturally.)

**Subtopic Keyword:** positioning strategy

**Search Intent:** Strategic. The reader isn't looking for a how-to on AI tools; they're deciding how prominent AI should be in their own story.
> **Keyword note:** "AI marketing" mostly draws searchers who want to *use* AI in marketing. This post is about *marketing a product that has AI in it*. It still fits CW's AI Marketing cluster and category, but don't expect it to rank for the head term on its own. Use the phrase in its "marketing claims about AI" sense, e.g., "AI marketing that leads with the outcome."

**Pillar Page:** [AI Studio](https://contentworkshop.com/solutions/ai-marketing/) (`/solutions/ai-marketing/`). This is what the recent AI Marketing posts link to: *Win the Search War*, *Google's Content Effort Score*, *Quality Inputs Matter*, and *We Built An AI Marketing Course*.

**Reader Problem:** Every competitor now leads with "AI-powered," so the word no longer differentiates anyone. Positioning that leads with AI reads the same as everyone else's and invites the trust and governance questions buyers care about most. Meanwhile, the outcome the buyer is paying for gets pushed below the fold.

**Content Angle (the claim):** For most B2B tech companies, AI belongs in the proof layer of positioning (how you deliver the outcome), not the headline. Leading with AI now signals sameness instead of innovation. AI-native categories where governance and trust are what buyers judge are the exception, and the post says so plainly.
> Drafting note: the claim above uses a "not X" construction for clarity in this brief only. The draft must stay within the house limit: zero in headings, one maximum in body copy.

**Proof / Example (anonymized):** Refer to the client only as "an emergency-response software company." Don't name it, link its case study, or include details that identify it (product names, the 700-page site count, the four audience names together).
- **What to use:** The site was product-first. It was organized around individual tools when it needed to be organized around the outcomes buyers cared about. CW rebuilt it with audience-first architecture and outcome-led copy (e.g., cutting charting time for field staff, giving operations leaders better incident visibility).
- **The parallel the post draws:** leading with AI is the new leading with features. Both put the mechanism ahead of the outcome.
- **Writer's note (internal, not for publication):** the source is the ESO Web Studio case study (contentworkshop.com/work/eso/). Check details against it, and keep them generalized. It isn't an AI-positioning project, so present it as an analogy, not as an AI before-and-after.

**Length:** 1,500 words, 5–6 major sections.

**Proposed Structure:**
1. **Intro:** the sameness test. Line up five competitors' homepage headlines. If "AI-powered" is in most of them, it's no longer a position. (~150w)
2. **H2: Why AI Moved Into the Headline**: the pressure from boards, investors and launches, and why it made sense in 2023 and stopped working once everyone did it. (~250w)
3. **H2: Promise, Proof, Mechanism**: the three layers of a position, where AI belongs in each, and what buyers check at each layer. (~350w)
4. **H2: What the Shift Looks Like in Practice**: the anonymized client example, from product-first to outcome-first, with the AI parallel drawn out. Then a short, clearly illustrative before/after headline rewrite. (~300w)
5. **H2: When AI Should Lead**: AI-native categories (e.g., agentic SOC), where trust and governance are the buying criteria, and what the headline has to say then. (~200w)
6. **H2: Test It Before You Rewrite Everything**: run positioning as a hypothesis with a message-map variant, sales-call language and how AI answers describe you. Ends with the CTA. (~250w)

**CTA Direction:** Conclude the argument rather than bolting on a pitch. Direction: "If your positioning reads like everyone else's AI story, Content Workshop can help you rebuild it around the outcome your buyers are paying for, with AI where it earns its place." Link the AI Studio pillar. Optionally mention Content Studio's messaging work.

**Risks to manage in the draft:**
- Investors and AI-native clients reward AI-forward positioning. The exception section has to be real, not a token caveat.
- Don't imply readers don't understand their own market (house persona rule).
- Any "count of AI-powered claims" must be framed as an exercise unless someone actually counts.
